# JWST imaging

JWST images reach this project as MAST products: calibrated exposures and the mosaics the pipeline builds from them. This guide describes how a band becomes part of a sky band composite, and what was measured. The time-series route for eclipse mapping ([eclipse-mapping.md](eclipse-mapping.md)) shares the toolchain and MAST access; nothing else.

## Stages

1. **Pin.** `tools/objects/jwst/imaging/archive.mts <program id> <crds context> <level-3 obs_id>...` asks MAST for each level-3 observation's mosaic, its image3 association and the level-2 `_cal` exposures that association names. It writes them as an imaging program in `tools/objects/jwst/imaging/programs/`, each file by URI and size. The band comes from the product's filter and pupil ([bands.mts](../tools/objects/jwst/imaging/bands.mts)); NIRCam's long-wave narrow filters are FILTER F444W with the narrow filter in the pupil wheel. Digests are added the first time a file is downloaded.
2. **Reproduce.** `image3.mts <program> <band> <work>` re-runs the pipeline's level-3 stage (calwebb_image3: tweakreg, skymatch, outlier detection, resample) from those exposures. It uses the pinned toolchain ([toolchain.json](../tools/objects/jwst/toolchain.json), jwst 2.0.1) and the program's CRDS context. Every exposure is checked against its pinned digest before the pipeline reads it, and the run writes a product record beside the mosaic ([product-record.ts](../packages/telescope/src/product-record.ts), the shared `cssearth-telescope-product@1` form): the exposures it was given, the settings and CRDS context, the pipeline versions and the digest of the toolchain pins, and the mosaic with the units its own header states, with an empty evidence list. A mosaic whose record says this same run made it is not made again. `compare.mts` then checks the result against MAST's own mosaic at the same sky positions, writes a receipt beside the program, and adds the agreement to that record as `archive-agreement` evidence.
   A NIRCam coronagraph band ([bands.mts](../tools/objects/jwst/imaging/bands.mts) names its focal-plane mask, which `archive.mts` reads from the subarray in the observation's name) is pinned from a coron3 association instead: the target's per-integration `_calints` exposures at each telescope roll, and the PSF reference star's as `references`. `coron3.mts <program> <band> <work>` re-runs calwebb_coron3 from them: stack the references, align them to each integration, subtract the star by KLIP, flag outliers, and resample the rolls into one mosaic. `compare.mts` checks it the same way, and also by distance from the star, since how well the subtraction matches changes with separation.
3. **Resample once.** With `--grid <recipe.json>` the same stage writes straight onto a sky band recipe's TAN grid, north up at the recipe's pixel scale. The exposures are drizzled once, instead of drizzled by MAST and resampled again.
   `--grid` is image3's only; `coron3.mts` writes the pipeline's own grid.
4. **Compose.** A sky band recipe names a JWST band in one of two ways. `{ band, program, sciSha256 }` uses the stage-3 mosaic on the recipe grid. It is pinned by the digest of its SCI data, because its headers carry run dates and paths. `{ band, product, sha256, bytes }` uses MAST's level-3 mosaic, which the composer resamples onto the grid through `@cssearth/fits` `skyProjection`. Both are MJy/sr as the pipeline calibrated them. The composer then applies the usual background and peak percentiles and the shared asinh display ([color-preparation.md](color-preparation.md#sky-survey-bands)). `pointSources: "mask"` reports stars as no coverage, for a lens that places the image in depth.
5. **Depth.** An image gets depth only from a fitted three-dimensional model: `packages/bake/src/density/column-depth.ts` spreads each sky column over the model's depth profile for that column. The column keeps its measured value, and every channel shares the profile, so the image is reproduced from the front. A column the model leaves empty is dropped and its share is reported. Nothing is extruded.
6. **Attach.** Coronagraphy shows what surrounds one star, so its images belong to that star rather than to the sky. [`tools/objects/circumstellar/author.mts <id>`](../tools/objects/circumstellar/author.mts) turns a pinned coronagraph band into a density-grid volume attached to the host body, the delivery [Betelgeuse's circumstellar volumes](../src/objects/betelgeuse-shell/README.md) use: no catalogue entry, the star's own frame, one unit per astronomical unit, listed among the star's datasets. It works from one recipe, `source/circumstellar.json`. The mosaic is read about the star through its own WCS (a coronagraph mosaic is rotated against north) and drawn in the reflectance colour its publisher shows: each band over the star's own flux, averaged into red, green and blue, with the stretch the published figure does not print fitted to that figure by [`fit-figure-stretch.mts`](../tools/objects/circumstellar/fit-figure-stretch.mts). [`disc-envelope.mts`](../tools/objects/circumstellar/disc-envelope.mts) measures the ring's ridge ellipse and refuses it if it departs from the published geometry the recipe cites. The drawn envelope is a disc of that geometry whose surface density follows the image's own deprojected radial profile; it must beat a single ring, a spherical shell and constant depth. The drawn extent comes from the image too: it tapers from where the ring's deprojected median falls to the noise, and a grid that would cut light above the noise is refused. Step 5 then spreads each column along the ring. Which side of the ring is nearer and how thick it is are stated in the recipe as conventions, and so is the one renderer setting a source cannot give, how opaque the top of the colour bar is, which is chosen by matching the rendered view from Earth to the published figure.

## Measured

On NGC 3132 (programme 2733), NIRCam F470N, eight long-wave exposures:

- **Reproduction** ([receipt](../tools/objects/jwst/imaging/programs/ngc-3132-2733.NIRCAM-F470N.reproduction.json)). The re-run has MAST's grid shape and rotation. Its reference point sits within 0.21 pixel of MAST's. Up to the 99.9th brightness percentile, its median brightness is within 0.2% of MAST's in every bin; star cores differ by 0.4%. Pixel by pixel the RMS difference is 6% of the RMS brightness, because the alignment differs at the sub-pixel level. Aligning absolutely to Gaia DR3 changes the grid (2357 × 2347 against MAST's 2356 × 2348), so MAST did not.
- **Determinism.** Two runs onto the same 1024 × 1024 grid produce identical SCI, ERR and WHT data. The files differ only in their headers.
- **Two routes.** F470N through the stage-3 grid mosaic and through MAST's mosaic resampled by the composer agree: background 0.473 against 0.474 MJy/sr, peak 62.44 against 62.18 MJy/sr, display correlation 0.9998. The stage-3 route leaves 1,090 edge pixels uncovered that MAST's mosaic covers.
- **Cost.** The stage peaks at 2.3 GiB of resident memory and takes 80–95 s for eight exposures. `image3.mts` stops a run that passes its ceiling (3 GiB by default) and does not start without twice the ceiling free. Keeping the exposure library on disk (`in_memory: false`) is what brings outlier detection under that ceiling. MAST's own F187N mosaic composes onto a 1024 × 1024 grid in 2.3 s at 0.59 GB, reading only the rows the grid covers.

On Europa (programme 1250, GTO, PI G. Villanueva), MIRI F1280W on the SUB64 subarray, four exposures, 40.5 s in total:

- **Reproduction** ([receipt](../tools/objects/jwst/imaging/programs/europa-1250.MIRI-F1280W.reproduction.json)). The re-run is on MAST's grid, 98 x 94 pixels, with every WCS card equal. Of the 6,183 pixels both cover, 53.4% are bit-identical, and no pixel is covered by only one of them. Above the median brightness the RMS difference is 9.6 x 10^-8 of the RMS brightness and the correlation is 1 to fourteen digits. The median ratio to MAST is exactly 1 in every brightness bin, up to and including the two brightest pixels. 12 s at 0.32 GiB. This is the first MIRI picture reduced here; the mode's bands were defined but unproven.

On HIP 65426 (programme 1386), NIRCam F444W behind MASK335R, two rolls and nine PSF reference exposures:

- **Reproduction** ([receipt](../tools/objects/jwst/imaging/programs/hip-65426-1386.NIRCAM-F444W-MASK335R.reproduction.json)). The re-run is on MAST's grid, with the same shape, reference pixel and blank pixels. Correlation with MAST's mosaic is 0.9966 at 0.5–1″ from the star, 0.984 at 1–2″, 0.917 at 2–5″ and 0.995 at 5–20″. Inside 0.5″, under the mask, it is 0.66. HIP 65426 b, the brightest source beyond 0.5″ (0.78″ from the star), keeps 98.2% of MAST's flux in a 7 × 7 pixel box. The outlier-flagged exposures are identical to MAST's. The PSF-subtracted rolls are not, but they leave the same residual (RMS 0.2796 against 0.2799 MJy/sr in the first roll); the pixel differences arise in alignment and KLIP. Platform numerics are a likely cause, but that is not verified.
- **Determinism.** Two runs give identical SCI data.
- **Cost.** 17 s at 0.66 GiB resident memory.

On HD 181327 (programme 2780), six filters behind MASK335R, drawn as [hd-181327-disc](../src/objects/hd-181327-disc/README.md):

- **Reproduction** (receipts `hd-181327-2780.NIRCAM-*-MASK335R.reproduction.json`). All six on MAST's grid, every alignment fit converged, 14–44 s at 0.64–0.91 GiB. Correlation with MAST above the median brightness is 0.961–0.996; at 1–2″, where the ring is, 0.887–0.987; at 0.5–1″ only 0.46–0.86, so nothing is drawn inside 1″.
- **Ring geometry, measured on the reflectance mean:** radius 80.8 au, inclination 28.6°, nodes at position angle 100.7°, against 80 au, 28.54° ± 0.31° and 100.39° ± 0.63° in Gáspár et al. (2026). One binning of the ridge alone moves the position angle by up to 5°, so nine are combined.
- **Depth.** The measured-profile disc leaves 0.456 against a signal of 2.50; a single ring leaves 0.532, a spherical shell 0.654 and constant depth 1.131.
- **Against the published figure.** The paper's Figure 1 (reflectance colour) is reproduced by the sky image with a colour error of 12.6/255 and luminance correlation 0.990 once its unprinted stretch is fitted; rendered from Earth, the ring's long axis lies 77.9° clockwise from up with axis ratio 0.875, against 78.5° and 0.876 (a mirror: 101.5°).
- **What failed first.** A composite on the sky-band display with M8's asinh settings drew the ring at 70–100% of its peak from 48 to 120 au where the data fall to a fifth. A disc 4 au thick, under the renderer's 5.7 au slice spacing, showed straight strips and a hard edge when tilted; it is drawn 8 au thick, which the images cannot tell apart (0.6%).

**What the archive holds** ([`coronagraphy-survey.mts`](../tools/objects/jwst/imaging/coronagraphy-survey.mts), 18 September 2026): 285 public level-3 NIRCam coronagraph observations of 79 targets. 276 resolve to one of 38 bands from their name; the other 9 are full-frame and name no occulter. Every one has a coron3 association with PSF reference exposures; their members total 228 GB, 175 MB at the median and 14 GB at most.

MIRI's four-quadrant phase masks do not reproduce, measured on HIP 65426 F1140C (observation c1021). The level-2 exposures and their outlier flags are identical to MAST's, but the PSF-subtracted rolls are not: ours keep a residual RMS of 1.59 MJy/sr against MAST's 1.14, and their mosaic correlates at 0.60 at 5–20″. The pipeline's alignment step fits each reference slice with scipy's least squares at a tolerance of 1e-15. In the first roll all 90 slices stop at the 800-evaluation limit, at y-shifts from −0.45 to −1.42 pixels; across both rolls 171 of 180 fits do (NIRCam: 0 of 36). Refitted at a tolerance of 1e-8, slices with near-equal cost settle anywhere from −0.26 to −1.32 pixels, and one still does not converge. The data do not fix the shift, so the mosaic depends on where each search stopped. `coron3.mts` therefore fails a run with any unconverged alignment, and no MIRI coronagraph band is defined.

## Spectral cubes

An integral-field unit records a small picture with a spectrum in every pixel. MAST's level-3 product is a cube (`_s3d`): surface brightness on axes (x, y, wavelength), north up. NIRSpec's pixels are 0.1″ and its field is 3″, so only a body several pixels across gets a map. Of the shipped bodies JWST has cubes of, ten are: Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Ganymede, Callisto, Io, Europa, Titan and Ceres. The rest fit inside one pixel and give one spectrum of the whole disc ([JWST ledger](jwst-ledger.md)).

Both integral-field units are read the same way. MIRI's medium-resolution spectrometer works differently from NIRSpec: it has no filter wheel, and one exposure sets a grating wheel to a sub-band (SHORT, MEDIUM or LONG) and reads two channels at once, channels 1 and 2 on the MIRIFUSHORT detector and 3 and 4 on MIRIFULONG. A cube is one channel in one sub-band, so a single visit yields twelve of them, covering 5 to 28 µm at a plate scale that grows with wavelength. Its bands are named `MIRI-MRS-CH<n>-<sub-band>`.

1. **Pin.** `archive.mts` pins a cube observation like a picture: the level-3 cube, its spec3 association and every level-2 `_cal` exposure that association names. For NIRSpec that is both detectors at each dither; for MIRI it is both detectors at each dither in each of the three grating settings, twenty-four exposures for one cube. All of them are pinned even though the cube is drizzled from one channel of one setting, because a moving target's frame is the mean sky position of every science exposure in the association (jwst 2.0.1, `assign_mtwcs`): leaving the other detector out moves the grid, measured below. The band is the instrument's setting ([bands.mts](../tools/objects/jwst/imaging/bands.mts), e.g. `NIRSPEC-G395H-F290LP`, `MIRI-MRS-CH1-SHORT`).
2. **Reproduce.** [`cubes/spec3.mts <program> <band> <work>`](../tools/objects/jwst/cubes/spec3.mts) re-runs calwebb_spec3 (outlier detection between exposures, then cube_build) on the pinned toolchain, compares the cube with MAST's sample by sample and writes a receipt beside the program. The two cubes are on one grid or the comparison fails. Before the pipeline reads anything, each member is checked against its pinned digest and its own header is read: it must be an integral-field exposure of the band's instrument, and the band's detector and sub-band must be among them. A MIRI run asks cube_build for the band's channel and sub-band alone, so it builds one cube rather than the association's twelve; the one-dimensional extraction is skipped, and with it MIRI's spectral-leak correction, which only ever changes an extracted spectrum. The run writes the same product record a mosaic gets, and the comparison adds its `archive-agreement` evidence to it.
3. **Measure.** [`cubes/spectral-cube.mts`](../tools/objects/jwst/cubes/spectral-cube.mts) reads a cube one plane at a time and measures an absorption band in every pixel: 1 − band / continuum, the continuum a straight line between a window on each side, read at the band's centre. It is a ratio of neighbouring wavelengths in one pixel, so it needs no solar spectrum, distance or albedo. Its error is carried from the cube's ERR planes.
4. **Place on the body.** [`cubes/author-body-maps.mts <object id>`](../tools/objects/jwst/cubes/author-body-maps.mts) works from one record, `source/preparation/jwst-band-maps.json`. Where each pixel lies on the body follows from when the cube was exposed (its header), where JWST and the Sun were (JPL Horizons, observer `500@-170`, pinned in the package) and how the body was turned (the IAU rotation model in `pck00011.tpc`). [`observer-camera.mts`](../tools/objects/terrestrial-layers/observer-camera.mts), the route the ground-based photographs use, turns those into a camera. JWST points to about one pixel, so the disc's centre is fitted: a disc of the body's known angular radius, blurred by a Gaussian, against the continuum image. The map is written as a full-world longitude-latitude FITS grid, empty where the body was not seen or was seen more obliquely than the record's limit, and the body's scalar-map lens reads it like any other.

### Measured on Europa, MIRI

Programme 1250 (GTO, PI G. Villanueva), MIRI MRS channel 1 SHORT, the first of the twelve cubes that visit produced:

- **Reproduction** ([receipt](../tools/objects/jwst/imaging/programs/europa-1250.MIRI-MRS-CH1-SHORT.reproduction.json)). The re-run cube is on MAST's grid, 45 x 41 x 1050. Of the 1,166,586 samples, 99.995% are bit-identical, none is covered by only one of the two cubes, the correlation is 1 to fifteen digits, and the largest relative difference among samples above the median brightness is 6.9 x 10^-8. 56 s at 3.3 GiB for twenty-four exposures.
- **Read.** The same reader the NIRSpec cubes use opens it unchanged: surface brightness in MJy/sr on a north-up tangent plane at 0.130 arcsecond per pixel, with a linear wavelength axis from 4.9004 to 5.7396 µm in 1,050 planes. The plate scale is the cube's own, from the CRDS `cubepar` reference the pipeline applied; nothing here declares one.
- **What failed first.** The first run pinned only the twelve MIRIFUSHORT exposures, on the argument that the cube is drizzled from one channel and that outlier detection works one detector at a time. The cube came out the same shape but on a grid shifted by 3.4 x 10^-4 arcsecond, 0.3% of a pixel: `assign_mtwcs` sets a moving target's frame to the mean sky position of every science exposure in the association, so the twelve MIRIFULONG exposures move it too. The whole association is pinned, and the comparison then agrees.

### Measured on Europa, NIRSpec

Programme 1250 (GTO, PI G. Villanueva), NIRSpec G395H, 23 November 2022, four exposures:

- **Reproduction** ([receipt](../tools/objects/jwst/imaging/programs/europa-1250.NIRSPEC-G395H-F290LP.reproduction.json)). The re-run cube is on MAST's grid (49 × 53 × 3610). 99.987% of its 4.77 million samples are bit-identical, the rest agree to 1.2 parts in ten million, and the band-depth maps differ by at most 6 × 10⁻⁹. 118 s at 3.9 GiB.
- **Camera.** Computed here: JWST over 2.69° N, 93.73° W, north azimuth 25.54°, the Sun over 83.43° W. Horizons' own sub-observer quantities for that instant, which this route does not read: 2.70° N, 93.73° W, north pole at position angle 334.46° (−25.54°), Sun over 83.43° W.
- **Disc.** 9.75 pixels across. The fitted centre is 0.04 pixel from the brightness centroid and 1.1 pixels from the header's target position; the fit leaves 2.5% of the peak.
- **Map.** The 4.25 µm carbon dioxide band is 4–17% deep over the 29% of the surface seen within 65° of the disc's centre (median error 0.1%). It is deepest at 8.5° S, 84.5° W. [Trumbo & Brown (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adg4155) and [Villanueva et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adg4270) report the band concentrated in Tara Regio, which the IAU nomenclature pinned in Europa's package centres at 10° S, 75° W and bounds at 42–107° W, 35° S–20° N. A map mirrored about the sub-observer meridian would put the peak near 103° W.

## Limits

- A cube map's detail is the telescope's: Europa's disc was ten pixels across, so nothing under about 300 km is surface detail. Both integral-field units are read; MIRI's cubes are pinned one channel and sub-band at a time.
- The re-run trusts MAST's level-2 exposures. Stages 1 and 2 are not re-run.
- Coronagraphy is NIRCam's only, and only subarray observations: a full-frame coronagraph observation's name does not say which occulter it is behind. MIRI's four-quadrant phase-mask alignment does not converge (above).
- Distortion-corrected mosaics are required. `skyProjection` refuses SIP, TPV and PV cards rather than approximating them.
- Detail finer than the depth model's cells has no depth of its own. It sits wherever the model puts matter along its column.
