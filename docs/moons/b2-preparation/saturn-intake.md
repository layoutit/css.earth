# B2 Saturn source intake

Intake baseline: `98ef3269a6f965cbf7d1434d6484876953f24eba`, branch `feat/moons-scientific-surfaces`. This is a source and implementation contract, not a completed rendering qualification. Exact downloaded bytes and dates are recorded in `saturn-acquisition.json`, `saturn-extra-acquisition.json` and `saturn-titan-final-acquisition.json`.

All five bodies have substantive downloadable products. The four icy moons currently render on spheres; Enceladus already has the Schenk 2024 elevation view, while Tethys, Dione and Rhea already have 2025 SPC radius-grid elevation views. Reusing those views alone would not satisfy B2. Titan currently has visible and radar imagery but no numeric topography.

## Enceladus

The [NAIF Cassini DSK archive](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/pds/data/co-s_j_e_v-spice-6-v1.0/cosp_1000/data/dsk/) supplies the actual corrected binary, label and [release explanation](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/pds/data/co-s_j_e_v-spice-6-v1.0/cosp_1000/data/dsk/dskinfo.txt). The selected v2 binary is 30,258,176 bytes; its source label was created 2026-06-11 and its embedded delivery note is dated 2025-10-21. These are release/processing dates, not imaging dates.

**Do not use v1 or the convenient 2024 Zenodo spherical-harmonic derivative.** NAIF explicitly reports an early processing error in v1 and reprocessing of the Cassini images for v2. The Zenodo model was derived from the original v1 DSK and therefore does not close this corrected-source requirement.

CSPICE N0067 / SpiceyPy 6.0.3 independently reads 396,294 stored vertices and 786,432 plates. This is a type-2 DSK, data class 1, surface 20122, target 602, frame 10040 (`IAU_ENCELADUS`), latitudinal coordinates, distances in kilometres. The stored vertex count includes duplicated ICQ patch boundaries; it must not be replaced by the theoretical welded count without verifying the exact duplicate map. The descriptor spans −180° to +180° longitude and −90° to +90° latitude, with radial bounds 247.68912735585084–257.59813481361255 km. These radial bounds are not heights relative to the Schenk ellipsoid.

The embedded source note places Salih crater at −5° east longitude and recommends `enceladus_ssd_250728_v2.tpc`, `cas_v44.tf`, SAT441 and DE440. The first two small frame files are retained. The global source family is described as 500 m resolution with local 25–100 m products; the selected Q256 version is a lower-density delivered model. Do not advertise the selected mesh as a 25 m or uniform 500 m product. The DSK’s 1950–2050 time interval is a kernel validity envelope, not temporal terrain coverage.

Existing Schenk 2024 elevations are in kilometres relative to the ellipsoid with **semi-axes** 256.2 × 251.4 × 248.6 km. Keep that numeric view and its title. Do not add those values to a mean sphere or assume they equal JPL radial heights. Independent anchors: source DSK radial intersections at signed cardinal longitudes/poles; selected source vertices and plates; Salih prime-meridian control; and explicitly datum-aware differences against the independent Schenk DEM. The existing ISS mosaic is registered cartography but its control network is not identical to the new JPL solution, so registration needs visible feature checks before qualification.

Plan: a pinned, reproducible CSPICE extraction that welds only exactly coincident vertices, retains a source-to-output vertex map, and feeds the existing OBJ reader. No new renderer or invented relief. Conversion/reduction accuracy and final face counts remain to be measured.

## Tethys, Dione and Rhea

These are released [Tethys](https://sbn.psi.edu/pds/resource/weirichtethysshape.html), [Dione](https://sbn.psi.edu/pds/resource/weirichdioneshape.html) and [Rhea](https://sbn.psi.edu/pds/resource/weirichrheashape.html) PDS bundles, all V1.0, released 2025-08-28. Dataset DOIs: `10.26033/hpv0-eh61`, `10.26033/bxx6-g543`, `10.26033/tqxb-q714`. They are attributed to Weirich, Gaskell, Palmer and Domingue (2025). Exact labels and bundle descriptions accompany the downloaded products.

The OBJ labels specify kilometres, 98,306 vertices and 196,608 facets per Q128 release. The body-fixed +X axis is longitude 0° and +Z is the positive/north pole. The Q128 mesh spacings are approximately 5.96 km (Tethys), 6.32 km (Dione), and 8.6 km (Rhea); these are coarser than the Q512-derived existing elevation grids (approximately 1.5, 1.6 and 2.15 km). New terrain geometry will therefore be described as a bounded reduction of the delivered shape, not a higher-resolution replacement for the existing scalar elevation map.

The new GeoTIFFs are the released Q512-derived **relative SPC albedo**, dimensionless, approximately 0–2 with mean near 1 by the source definition. Converting them to geometric albedo or calibrated reflectance requires additional photometric modelling. The source explicitly says albedo is a secondary SPC product whose accuracy has not been examined as thoroughly as topography. Tethys SPC uses uncalibrated ISS images; Dione and Rhea use calibrated 32-bit ISS images. This distinction will remain in source documentation.

Tethys and Dione each use an equatorial equirectangular product (−55° to +55°, center longitude 180°) plus north and south polar stereographic products (center longitude 0°, latitude of origin ±90°). The exact reference radii are 531,000 m and 561,400 m. These grids have the same dimensions and georeference as their respective already-qualified radius products. Their small source join gaps must remain missing. Rhea uses a global equirectangular grid centered at 180° on a 763,500 m reference sphere. Full label/geokey comparisons and independent pixel-center anchors will validate their sampling.

Numeric `*_sigma_c.cub` and `*_numimg_c.cub` plus assessment PDFs are retained for honest quality evidence. Sigma is **maplet agreement/internal consistency**, not absolute height error. Global maplet FormU values in the source descriptions are 892.0 m (Tethys), 187 m (Dione) and 286.4 m (Rhea); none is a universal per-pixel accuracy claim. The expected 1–2 GSD topographic-error guideline derives from validation elsewhere and is not an independent measurement of every moon vertex.

Independent anchor opportunities: native XYZ cardinal rays and exact released facets; raw numeric albedo cells in all equatorial/polar projections; checks of no-data seams and pole convergence; comparison with the separately retained radius GeoTIFFs; actual maplet-quality extrema and image counts. Rendering legends must say “Relative SPC albedo” and use dimensionless units.

The public PDS download endpoint rejects Python’s default User-Agent with HTTP403/code1010. A descriptive `cssEarth source verification (public PDS data)` User-Agent returns HTTP200 for exact public files. Record that header in acquisition operations; the initial 403 was an access limitation, not absent data.

## Titan

The [Cornell Cassini RADAR GTDR archive](https://data.astro.cornell.edu/RADAR/DATA/GTDR/) has explicit formal PDS labels under dataset `CO-SSA-RADAR-5-GTDR-V1.0`, produced by Paul Corlies/Cornell in 2019. Selected pairs are `GTFED00N{090,270}_T126_V01` (measured height), `GTIED00N{090,270}_T126_V01` (interpolation), and `GTDED00N{090,270}_T126_V01` (distance to nearest measurement). Actual compressed image and label bytes are retained; no press-map reconstruction is involved.

The formal labels close the datum: measured/interpolated elevation is in metres relative to a **2575.0 km reference sphere**; distance is in kilometres. Height inputs are adjusted altimetry and SARtopo. Do not claim these formal GTDR fields also contain the 19 stereo DTMs of the 2017 paper merely because the paper describes those. The earlier Cornell `topo_4PPD*.cub` and 26 MB zip were downloaded for comparison but are superseded as the implementation choice: their embedded labels do not directly specify elevation datum, and their fine georeference differs from the formal GTDR delivery.

Selected GTDR files are 1440 × 1440 per hemisphere, 8 pixels/degree (about 5.618 km posting). They have float32 little-endian (`PC_REAL`) values, identity scale/offset and bit-exact missing constant `FF7FFFFB`. Detached labels identify the corresponding attached-label `.IMG` file. The product projection is equirectangular, west-positive, planetographic on a sphere. Planetographic and planetocentric latitudes coincide for this specified spherical datum; convert west longitude to east exactly. The `090` and `270` names refer to west-longitude hemisphere centers, not the atlas’s east-longitude centers.

The science views will make their support explicit: measured-height gaps remain missing; interpolation is a model estimate between tracks; the distance view shows support spacing and is **not uncertainty**. Grid posting is not measurement footprint size. No fictitious coverage or exaggerated geometric terrain is needed. The 2017 paper’s approximate 9% combined measurement area must not be asserted as a measured statistic of this distinct formal delivery. We will compute the actual area-weighted valid coverage of the selected GTDR floats.

Independent anchors: direct float bytes and missing-bit tests; inverse PDS sample/line projection at signed cardinal longitudes; both hemisphere joins; source measured/interpolated differences without assuming equality after different processing steps; distance values compared against selected nearby measured footprints as a qualitative support check. The [Cassini RADAR User’s Guide, Figure4-9](https://pds.nasa.gov/data/pds4/misc/document_cassini/Cassini_RADAR_Users_Guide_2nd_Ed_191004_cmp_200421.pdf) separately identifies observed height, interpolation and distance as distinct GTDR products and confirms the sphere datum. [Corlies et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017GL075518) explains the interpolation methodology and its limitations. The publisher’s supplementary downloads currently return403 to the direct client; this does not block the formally labelled PDS products.

## Terms, implementation and remaining gates

PDS/NAIF data are public scientific archive products; preserve dataset authors, DOIs, mission credit, XML/PDS labels and provenance. No explicit Creative Commons license appears in these downloaded individual labels. Do not invent one. The source scientific papers remain copyrighted; use concise original interpretation and citations, not republished article text. The earlier Cornell research cubes similarly expose no explicit standalone reuse license in their listing, another reason to use the formal mission archive products. PDS data-use policy is the applicable archive context; retain its link in package NOTICE files.

The required shared changes are preparation-only: a narrowly validated PDS float-map decoder dispatch, and a specific pinned DSK-to-mesh acquisition operation. The source-owned recipes contain units, scales, projections, caveats and product selection. Existing rendering, input policy and shared shell remain generic. A conversion, bake, successful test or download alone is not final scientific/visual acceptance. Per-body independent anchors, bounded geometric error, source restoration, production build and real Chrome DPR1/2 remain mandatory before readiness.

No large bake has run. All heavier preparation/test/browser jobs require the root’s serialized scheduling slot, a current disk/memory-pressure check and an output-volume statement. No new worktree, remote publication or cleanup is authorized.
