ASTEROID DIMENSIONS FROM OCCULTATIONS

John Broughton - updated 2018-04-30


    Presenting results for 520 asteroids, these tables are based on procedures formulated in 2011-2012 to combine diverse occultation data sourced from archived observations. Listed for comparison are the IRAS and AcuA radiometric diameters, followed by the derived mean diameter and geometric albedo. Adjacent to albedo is taxonomic classification in the Tholen system. In the events column, occultation totals are split into two distinct categories requiring different procedures in the calculation of event diameters. Category 1 represents the number of occultations with fitted ellipse plots, whereas category 2 tallies those with sparsely-observed chords. The reduction requires a diameter from each event to be combined in deriving the mean diameter and albedo. Double and single weight is applied according to category, except for short chords of category 2 where weight is fractional. Ellipsoid dimensions are based on limits determined by fitted-ellipse parameters and the longest chord, with the mean diameter playing a role in deriving the third dimension. Out of several thousand chords, 13 appear to be spurious due to their excessive length and have been excluded from the calculations. The only other exclusions apply to all-but-one Ceres occultation, as detailed in the Notes.

Selection criteria
     To qualify for the table of stage-1 asteroids requires one category-1 occultation (fitted ellipse) or at least three of category 2 (chord-length based). 1-sigma uncertainties in mean diameter are typically in the 5-15% range at that stage. Asteroids with at least two category-1 occultations are promoted to the stage 2 table, progressing uncertainty into the sub-5% range. Radiometric or occultation diameters in stage 2 are greyed when they are unlikely to be the best of the available figures; depending on consistency between the radiometric figures, the number and consistency of contributing event diameters, the relationship between taxonomic class and albedo, and other evidence. Such judgements are not based on error margins, given there are often much larger discrepancies between radiometric diameters in comparison with their stated margins. The 28 main-belt asteroids from both tables with derived mean diameters exceeding 200km are sorted in order of decreasing diameter in the table of Large MBAs - an apparent scarcity in the 185-200km range makes this a rational minimum size to distinguish them as large MBAs.

(1) Ceres  
    Being a fully-differentiated dwarf planet, Ceres has been treated differently than the rest of the asteroid population. Its highly-regular shape allows dimensions to be measured within 1% accuracy from just one well-observed 1984 occultation, and cannot be improved on by combining with lesser-quality observations. However, even the 1984 chords depart too much from a fitted ellipse to be taken at face value. An issue with the initial fitted ellipse (960*908) is a smaller equatorial to polar difference of 52km compared with a theoretical prediction of 64km that was independently confirmed by HST observations. Note that the equatorial tilt in 1984 was insignificant during the occultation and amounts to only a half-kilometre increase in the apparent polar diameter. Regarding the reported timings (maximum duration 75.3s), chord #12 was acknowledged to have an absolute uncertainty of 1s in a 1987 paper dealing with the analysis, and I've taken the view that individual timings derived for the other chords are likely to have an accuracy no better than 0.5, given the small 0.07 magnitude drop being measured and the timing residuals involved. Therefore, time shifts of 1s for chord #12 and 0.5 for several others were applied to reduce the departures from a figure of revolution. For Occult users who wish to investigate the modifications themselves, the time shifts applied were:
Chord #1 +0.5 - both D and R
Chord #3 -0.5 - both D and R
Chord #7 +0.5 - R only
Chord #10 -0.5 - D only
Chord #12 -1.0 - both D and R
The resulting dimensions of 964*964*904 now have an equatorial-to-polar difference more in line with prediction. Subsequently the Dawn mission measured dimensions of 965.2*961.2*891.2 ±2.0.

(4) Vesta
    The mean diameter of Vesta from Dawn mission results is 525.4±0.2 km (572.6*557.2*446.4). The occultation diameter differs by only 4%, despite the limited number of observations Vesta has had at its current stage-1 status.

(65) Cybele  
    Observations prior to 2014 were puzzling. The only multiple-chord event produced a well-constrained three-chord ellipse of 245*198km in 1979, and the length of the other single chords were no greater than the derived mean diameter of 207km. If Cybele has the 0.05 albedo typical of its taxonomic class and outer main-belt location, its diameter ought to be in the vicinity of 280km, ranking it in the top ten MBAs. Its brief six-hour rotation period probably rules it out as a double asteroid, and lightcurve amplitudes within a 0.12-mag range imply it is not particularly elongated, which puts the 1979 ellipse at odds with a mean diameter anywhere near 280km. UPDATE - as of 2017, four more occultations have been observed and the mean diameter has increased substantially to 239km, close to the IRAS value and moving it up to 14th in the table of large MBAs.
