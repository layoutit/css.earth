# Kepler-8 b

## Sources

It is the only planet known around Kepler-8. Its orbit and size follow Esteves et al. 2015's fit, the archive's default. The introduction is generated from Esteves et al. 2015's published values; the sections below are the data's own.

**Size and mass.** Radius 1.416 Jupiter radii from Esteves et al. 2015 (2015ApJ...804..150E), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...804..150E/abstract): 101,232.7 km at 71,492 km per Jupiter radius. GM from the mass 0.59 Jupiter masses (Esteves et al. 2015, the mass the NASA Exoplanet Archive's composite table adopts (2015ApJ...804..150E), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive, https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...804..150E/abstract) times JPL's Jupiter GM. A sphere: no oblateness is measured.

**Orbit.** Holczer et al. 2016 (2016ApJS..225....9H), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname HOLCZER_ET_AL__2016): P 3.52249865 d Esteves et al. 2015 (2015ApJ...804..150E), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname ESTEVES_ET_AL__2015): a/R* 6.854; Esteves et al. 2015 (2015ApJ...804..150E), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname ESTEVES_ET_AL__2015): inclination 83.978 degrees Jenkins et al. 2010 (2010ApJ...724.1108J), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname JENKINS_ET_AL__2010): e 0 Holczer et al. 2016 (2016ApJS..225....9H), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname HOLCZER_ET_AL__2016): transit mid-time 2454954.119425 BJD, taken as BJD_TDB; with its period, the row that predicts 2026-01-01 best (1 sigma 1 min) Display convention: transit photometry does not measure the orbit's position angle on the sky, so the ascending node is set at position angle 0 (celestial north).

**Color.** A black body at the 2,400 K dayside brightness temperature measured in secondary eclipse in the Kepler band (Esteves et al. 2015, dayside brightness temperature in the Kepler band, assuming no reflected light): #ffa041. Read from the paper (Table 5): the paper's value (+100, -200 K; the larger is kept) from an eclipse 26 ± 10 ppm deep; no infrared eclipse is published. Reflected starlight is not included.

**Charts.** The orbits of Kepler-8's planets from above, from their hosted-orbit records, and its transit in 3 TESS sectors (74, 80, 81), folded onto its orbit. Upper limits and rows without an error are left out.
