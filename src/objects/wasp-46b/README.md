# WASP-46 b

## Sources

It is the only planet known around WASP-46. Its orbit and size follow Ciceri et al. 2016's fit, the archive's default. The introduction is generated from Ciceri et al. 2016's published values; the sections below are the data's own.

**Size and mass.** Radius 1.174 Jupiter radii from Ciceri et al. 2016 (2016MNRAS.456..990C), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.456..990C/abstract): 83,931.6 km at 71,492 km per Jupiter radius. GM from the mass 1.91 Jupiter masses (Ciceri et al. 2016, the mass the NASA Exoplanet Archive's composite table adopts (2016MNRAS.456..990C), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive, https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.456..990C/abstract) times JPL's Jupiter GM. A sphere: no oblateness is measured.

**Orbit.** Kokori et al. 2023 (2023ApJS..265....4K), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname KOKORI_ET_AL__2023): P 1.43037192 d Ciceri et al. 2016 (2016MNRAS.456..990C), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname CICERI_ET_AL__2016): a/R* 5.851; Ciceri et al. 2016 (2016MNRAS.456..990C), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname CICERI_ET_AL__2016): inclination 82.8 degrees Anderson et al. 2011 (2011arXiv1105.3179A), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname ANDERSON_ET_AL__2011): e 0 Kokori et al. 2023 (2023ApJS..265....4K), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname KOKORI_ET_AL__2023): transit mid-time 2457715.24083 BJD, taken as BJD_TDB; with its period, the row that predicts 2026-01-01 best (1 sigma 1 min) Display convention: transit photometry does not measure the orbit's position angle on the sky, so the ascending node is set at position angle 0 (celestial north).

**Color.** A black body at the 2,200 K dayside brightness temperature measured in secondary eclipse at 2.159 µm (Zhou et al. 2015, dayside brightness temperature at 2.159 µm (NASA Exoplanet Archive emission table)): #ff962f. Chosen from the archive's emission rows by rule: 1 measured of 1 rows; the smallest relative uncertainty, then the longest wavelength. Reflected starlight is not included.

**Charts.** The orbits of WASP-46's planets from above, from their hosted-orbit records, and its transit in 3 TESS sectors (103, 104, 105), folded onto its orbit. Upper limits and rows without an error are left out.
