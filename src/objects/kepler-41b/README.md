# Kepler-41 b

## Sources

It is the only planet known around Kepler-41. Its orbit and size follow Bonomo et al. 2015's fit, the archive's default. The introduction is generated from Bonomo et al. 2015's published values; the sections below are the data's own.

**Size and mass.** Radius 1.29 Jupiter radii from Bonomo et al. 2015 (2015A&A...575A..85B), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015A&A...575A..85B/abstract): 92,224.7 km at 71,492 km per Jupiter radius. GM from the mass 0.56 Jupiter masses (Bonomo et al. 2015, the mass the NASA Exoplanet Archive's composite table adopts (2015A&A...575A..85B), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive, https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015A&A...575A..85B/abstract) times JPL's Jupiter GM. A sphere: no oblateness is measured.

**Orbit.** Holczer et al. 2016 (2016ApJS..225....9H), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname HOLCZER_ET_AL__2016): P 1.85555757 d Bonomo et al. 2015 (2015A&A...575A..85B), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname BONOMO_ET_AL__2015): a/R* 5.159; Bonomo et al. 2015 (2015A&A...575A..85B), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname BONOMO_ET_AL__2015): inclination 82.51 degrees Bonomo et al. 2015 (2015A&A...575A..85B), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname BONOMO_ET_AL__2015): e 0 Holczer et al. 2016 (2016ApJS..225....9H), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname HOLCZER_ET_AL__2016): transit mid-time 2454964.6145 BJD, taken as BJD_TDB; with its period, the row that predicts 2026-01-01 best (1 sigma 1 min) Display convention: transit photometry does not measure the orbit's position angle on the sky, so the ascending node is set at position angle 0 (celestial north).

**Color.** A black body at the 2,400 K dayside brightness temperature measured in secondary eclipse in the Kepler band (Esteves et al. 2015, dayside brightness temperature in the Kepler band, assuming no reflected light): #ffa041. Read from the paper (Table 6): the paper's value from an eclipse 44 ppm deep (+15, -16); no infrared eclipse is published. Reflected starlight is not included.

**Charts.** The orbits of Kepler-41's planets from above, from their hosted-orbit records, and its transit in 3 TESS sectors (75, 81, 82), folded onto its orbit. Upper limits and rows without an error are left out.
