# WASP-22 b

## Sources

It is the only planet known around Tojil. Its orbit and size follow Stassun et al. 2017's fit, the archive's default. The introduction is generated from Stassun et al. 2017's published values; the sections below are the data's own.

**Size and mass.** Radius 1.23 Jupiter radii from Stassun et al. 2017 (2017AJ....153..136S), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017AJ....153..136S/abstract): 87,935.2 km at 71,492 km per Jupiter radius. GM from the mass 0.67 Jupiter masses (Stassun et al. 2017, the mass the NASA Exoplanet Archive's composite table adopts (2017AJ....153..136S), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive, https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017AJ....153..136S/abstract) times JPL's Jupiter GM. A sphere: no oblateness is measured.

**Orbit.** Kokori et al. 2023 (2023ApJS..265....4K), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname KOKORI_ET_AL__2023): P 3.53272912 d Stassun et al. 2017 (2017AJ....153..136S), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname STASSUN_ET_AL__2017): a/R* 8.94; Stassun et al. 2017 (2017AJ....153..136S), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname STASSUN_ET_AL__2017): inclination 89.2 degrees Knutson et al. 2014 (2014ApJ...785..126K), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname KNUTSON_ET_AL__2014): e 0.0108 Knutson et al. 2014 (2014ApJ...785..126K), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname KNUTSON_ET_AL__2014): omega 114 degrees Kokori et al. 2023 (2023ApJS..265....4K), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname KOKORI_ET_AL__2023): transit mid-time 2456850.43601 BJD, taken as BJD_TDB; with its period, the row that predicts 2026-01-01 best (1 sigma 1 min) Display convention: transit photometry does not measure the orbit's position angle on the sky, so the ascending node is set at position angle 0 (celestial north).

**Color.** An estimate, not a measurement: a black body at the 1,502 K equilibrium temperature of Southworth et al. 2016, equilibrium temperature (NASA Exoplanet Archive planetary systems table): #ff6c00. Chosen by rule: 2 papers in the archive print one; the most recently published. On 120 hot Jupiters whose day side Spitzer measured, the measured temperature is within 20% of this kind of estimate for 83% (Deming et al. 2023); see [the expected glow](../../../docs/color-preparation.md#the-expected-glow-of-a-hot-giant). Reflected starlight is not included.

**Charts.** The orbits of Tojil's planets from above, from their hosted-orbit records, and its transit in 3 TESS sectors (105, 106, 107), folded onto its orbit. Upper limits and rows without an error are left out.

## Known problems

- **Orbit convention.** omega 114 degrees is taken as Knutson et al. 2014 gives it through the archive (pl_orblper); papers differ on whether that is the star's or the planet's argument of periastron. The epoch is the transit, so a swapped convention would only mirror the ellipse (e 0.0108) about the line of sight.
- **Quoted text.** The introduction quotes sentences of the Wikipedia article "WASP-22" (revision 1375720811) verbatim, CC BY-SA 4.0.
