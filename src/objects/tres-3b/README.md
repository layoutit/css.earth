# TrES-3 b

It is the only planet known around Pipoltr. Its orbit and size follow Sozzetti et al. 2009's fit, the archive's default. The introduction is generated from Sozzetti et al. 2009's published values; the sections below are the data's own.

**Size and mass.** Radius 1.336 Jupiter radii from Sozzetti et al. 2009 (2009ApJ...691.1145S), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...691.1145S/abstract): 95,513.3 km at 71,492 km per Jupiter radius. GM from the mass 1.91 Jupiter masses (Sozzetti et al. 2009, the mass the NASA Exoplanet Archive's composite table adopts (2009ApJ...691.1145S), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive, https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...691.1145S/abstract) times JPL's Jupiter GM. A sphere: no oblateness is measured.

**Orbit.** Sodickson & Grunblatt 2025 (2025ApJ...993...78S), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname SODICKSON__AMP__GRUNBLATT_2025): P 1.30618626966 d Sozzetti et al. 2009 (2009ApJ...691.1145S), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname SOZZETTI_ET_AL__2009): a/R* 5.926; Sozzetti et al. 2009 (2009ApJ...691.1145S), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname SOZZETTI_ET_AL__2009): inclination 81.85 degrees Sozzetti et al. 2009 (2009ApJ...691.1145S), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname SOZZETTI_ET_AL__2009): e 0 Sodickson & Grunblatt 2025 (2025ApJ...993...78S), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname SODICKSON__AMP__GRUNBLATT_2025): transit mid-time 2454185.91113531 BJD, taken as BJD_TDB; with its period, the row that predicts 2026-01-01 best (1 sigma 1 min) Display convention: transit photometry does not measure the orbit's position angle on the sky, so the ascending node is set at position angle 0 (celestial north).

**Color.** A black body at the 1,731 K dayside brightness temperature measured in secondary eclipse at 2.146 µm (Croll et al. 2010, dayside brightness temperature at 2.146 µm (NASA Exoplanet Archive emission table)): #ff7b00. Chosen from the archive's emission rows by rule: 1 measured of 6 rows; the smallest relative uncertainty, then the longest wavelength. Reflected starlight is not included.

**Charts.** The orbits of Pipoltr's planets from above, from their hosted-orbit records, and its transit in 3 TESS sectors (53, 79, 80), folded onto its orbit. Upper limits and rows without an error are left out.
