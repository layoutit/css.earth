# HAT-P-4 b

## Sources

It is the only planet known around HAT-P-4. Its orbit and size follow Torres et al. 2008's fit, the archive's default. The introduction is generated from Torres et al. 2008's published values; the sections below are the data's own.

**Size and mass.** Radius 1.274 Jupiter radii from Torres et al. 2008 (2008ApJ...677.1324T), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...677.1324T/abstract): 91,080.8 km at 71,492 km per Jupiter radius. GM from the mass 0.671 Jupiter masses (Torres et al. 2008, the mass the NASA Exoplanet Archive's composite table adopts (2008ApJ...677.1324T), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive, https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...677.1324T/abstract) times JPL's Jupiter GM. A sphere: no oblateness is measured.

**Orbit.** Kokori et al. 2023 (2023ApJS..265....4K), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname KOKORI_ET_AL__2023): P 3.05652301 d Torres et al. 2008 (2008ApJ...677.1324T), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname TORRES_ET_AL__2008): a/R* 6.04; Torres et al. 2008 (2008ApJ...677.1324T), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname TORRES_ET_AL__2008): inclination 89.91 degrees Torres et al. 2008 (2008ApJ...677.1324T), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname TORRES_ET_AL__2008): e 0 Kokori et al. 2023 (2023ApJS..265....4K), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname KOKORI_ET_AL__2023): transit mid-time 2455584.57238 BJD, taken as BJD_TDB; with its period, the row that predicts 2026-01-01 best (1 sigma 1 min) Display convention: transit photometry does not measure the orbit's position angle on the sky, so the ascending node is set at position angle 0 (celestial north).

**Color.** A black body at the 2,194 K dayside brightness temperature measured in secondary eclipse at 3.6 µm (Todorov et al. 2013, dayside brightness temperature at 3.6 µm (NASA Exoplanet Archive emission table)): #ff952e. Chosen from the archive's emission rows by rule: 2 measured of 3 rows; the smallest relative uncertainty, then the longest wavelength. Reflected starlight is not included.

**Charts.** The orbits of HAT-P-4's planets from above, from their hosted-orbit records, and its transit in 3 TESS sectors (51, 77, 78), folded onto its orbit. Upper limits and rows without an error are left out.
