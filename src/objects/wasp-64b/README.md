# WASP-64 b

## Sources

It is the only planet known around Atakoraka. Its orbit and size follow Gillon et al. 2013's fit, the archive's default. The introduction is generated from Gillon et al. 2013's published values; the sections below are the data's own.

**Size and mass.** Radius 1.271 Jupiter radii from Gillon et al. 2013 (2013A&A...552A..82G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013A%26A...552A..82G/abstract): 90,866.3 km at 71,492 km per Jupiter radius. GM from the mass 1.271 Jupiter masses (Gillon et al. 2013, the mass the NASA Exoplanet Archive's composite table adopts (2013A&A...552A..82G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive, https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013A%26A...552A..82G/abstract) times JPL's Jupiter GM. A sphere: no oblateness is measured.

**Orbit.** Kokori et al. 2023 (2023ApJS..265....4K), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname KOKORI_ET_AL__2023): P 1.573290273 d Gillon et al. 2013 (2013A&A...552A..82G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname GILLON_ET_AL__2013): a/R* 5.39; Gillon et al. 2013 (2013A&A...552A..82G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname GILLON_ET_AL__2013): inclination 86.57 degrees Gillon et al. 2013 (2013A&A...552A..82G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname GILLON_ET_AL__2013): e 0 Kokori et al. 2023 (2023ApJS..265....4K), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname KOKORI_ET_AL__2023): transit mid-time 2457133.866164 BJD, taken as BJD_TDB; with its period, the row that predicts 2026-01-01 best (1 sigma 1 min) Display convention: transit photometry does not measure the orbit's position angle on the sky, so the ascending node is set at position angle 0 (celestial north).

**Color.** No image or measured color exists. The neutral gray is lit by wasp-64's measured color (#ffeee5, the color dataset of wasp-64 (src/objects/wasp-64/source/photometry/stellar-color.json)) at the gray's own brightness.

**Charts.** The orbits of Atakoraka's planets from above, from their hosted-orbit records, and its transit in 3 TESS sectors (33, 34, 87), folded onto its orbit. Upper limits and rows without an error are left out.
