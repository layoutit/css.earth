# TOI-2049 b

## Sources

It is the only planet known around TOI-2049. Its orbit and size follow Guenther et al. 2026's fit, the archive's default. This account was drafted from Guenther et al. 2026's values; the sections below are the data's own.

**Size and mass.** Radius 1.485 Jupiter radii from Guenther et al. 2026 (2026AJ....172...54G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026AJ....172...54G/abstract): 106,165.6 km at 71,492 km per Jupiter radius. GM from the mass 1.53 Jupiter masses (Guenther et al. 2026, the mass the NASA Exoplanet Archive's composite table adopts (2026AJ....172...54G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive, https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026AJ....172...54G/abstract) times JPL's Jupiter GM. A sphere: no oblateness is measured.

**Orbit.** Guenther et al. 2026 (2026AJ....172...54G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname GUENTHER_ET_AL_2026): P 5.303468 d Guenther et al. 2026 (2026AJ....172...54G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname GUENTHER_ET_AL_2026): a/R* derived from its semi-major axis 0.06919 au and stellar radius 3.2 solar radii; Guenther et al. 2026 (2026AJ....172...54G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname GUENTHER_ET_AL_2026): inclination 78.85 degrees Guenther et al. 2026 (2026AJ....172...54G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname GUENTHER_ET_AL_2026): e 0.054 Guenther et al. 2026 (2026AJ....172...54G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname GUENTHER_ET_AL_2026): omega 39 degrees Guenther et al. 2026 (2026AJ....172...54G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname GUENTHER_ET_AL_2026): transit mid-time 2458768.846 BJD, taken as BJD_TDB Display convention: transit photometry does not measure the orbit's position angle on the sky, so the ascending node is set at position angle 0 (celestial north).

**Colour.** No image or measured colour exists. The neutral gray is lit by toi-2049's measured colour (#ffe7d5, the colour lens of toi-2049 (src/objects/toi-2049/source/photometry/stellar-color.json)) at the gray's own brightness.

## Known problems

- **Orbit convention.** omega 39 degrees is taken as Guenther et al. 2026 gives it through the archive (pl_orblper); papers differ on whether that is the star's or the planet's argument of periastron. The epoch is the transit, so a swapped convention would only mirror the ellipse (e 0.054) about the line of sight.
- **Drafted text.** The card and introduction were written by the generator from the cited values, not by a person.
