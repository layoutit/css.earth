# HD 189733b

## Sources

HD 189733b is a hot Jupiter that orbits the K dwarf [HD 189733 A](../hd-189733/README.md) every 2.2 days, 19.8 parsecs from the Sun. Its default dataset is the dayside brightness-temperature map Lally et al. (2025) fitted to eclipses at 8 µm. A second scientific dataset shows the deposited map uncertainty; the Charts tab shows measured reflected light and transmission through the atmosphere.

**The map.** Lally et al. (2025, [arXiv:2503.20895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20895)) fitted a two-dimensional map to two JWST MIRI/LRS eclipses (18 October 2022 and 30 June 2023, program GO 2021, PI B. Kilpatrick) together with seven Spitzer IRAC 8 µm eclipses and a partial Spitzer phase curve. As the planet passes behind its star, the star covers and uncovers the dayside strip by strip, and the light curve records which strips are bright. They used ThERESA's eigenmapping: fifth-degree spherical harmonics combined into two eigenmaps, chosen by BIC. They converted flux to brightness temperature with a PHOENIX stellar spectrum and a blackbody planet over the Spitzer 8 µm band. Their data deposit, [Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.15103479](https://zenodo.org/records/15103479) (CC BY 4.0), holds the best-fitting map from the Eureka! reduction of the MIRI data (`output_E.npy`) and its ThERESA configuration.

**Only observed longitudes.** An eclipse map constrains only the longitudes that faced the telescope while it watched. ThERESA's maps show only those: it takes the sub-observer longitude at every observation time, widens it by 90° each way, and keeps the cells that overlap the range ([`utils.vislon`](https://github.com/rychallener/ThERESA/blob/74a8fec0462f4583e336bbc44e2f2441b263a49f/theresa/lib/utils.py) and `theresa.py` at commit 74a8fec). The deposited `tmap` fills every cell, so preparation applies the same rule to the observation times saved in the same file. The range is −109.9° to +179.6°, so the four columns from −180° to −120° are left as missing data. The Spitzer partial phase curve is why most of the nightside is shown; the paper analyses only the dayside.

**Processing.** The deposit file is restored unchanged and read in place with the restricted pickle reader ([npy-pickle.ts](../../../packages/bake/src/objects/raster/numpy/npy-pickle.ts)). It stores values without grid arrays, so the recipe states the grid layout, `pixel-centres`: south to north and west to east from −180°, values at cell centres, as ThERESA builds it. [npy-dictionary-map.ts](../../../packages/bake/src/objects/raster/numpy/npy-dictionary-map.ts) samples it bilinearly; a cell beside a hidden column is drawn whole. The `terrestrial-scientific` science kind paints 950 to 1,350 K, the observed map's range rounded to 50 K, with the plasma palette, which is false colour. The body is drawn emissive: the map is the planet's own heat glow.

**Orbit and rotation.** The planet orbits its placed host on the circular orbit of the deposited configuration ([hostedOrbits.ts](../../../packages/astronomy/src/hostedOrbits.ts)). The rotation record, `cssearth-synchronous-rotation@1`, assumes tidal locking, as the paper does: the pole is the orbit normal and longitude 0 faces the star. The default camera looks at the substellar point with the pole up.

**Navigation marker.** From a distance the planet is its map seen from the host star, an orthographic dayside disc centred on the substellar point, rendered by [author.mts](../../../packages/telescope-cli/authoring/hd-189733/author.mts) (`--check` recomputes it).

**Illustration dataset.** NASA's artist's concept of HD 189733b: the map its [Eyes on Exoplanets](https://eyes.nasa.gov/apps/exo/) app wraps around the planet ([`HD_189733_b.jpg`](https://eyes.nasa.gov/apps/exo/assets/image/exoplanet/HD_189733_b.jpg), 2,048 × 1,024, named in the app's texture table), credited NASA/JPL-Caltech. NASA says each planet in the app shows "an artist's concept of what it might look like" ([tutorial](https://science.nasa.gov/tutorials/eyes-on-exoplanets-tutorial/)); the file carries no credit or date of its own, and NASA does not say how it was made. Nobody has resolved this planet's disc, so none of the colour, clouds or terrain in the map was observed. Preparation resizes it unchanged onto the sphere with its left edge at 0° longitude ([`equirectangular-illustration`](../../../packages/bake/src/objects/interpretation/interpret.ts)), so its longitudes are arbitrary. It is an optional dataset: MIRI stays the default. It is listed in the package's illustration datasets, so it never counts as imagery. The planet is drawn self-luminous, so the map is shown evenly bright, without its star's shading, as the MIRI dataset is. NASA content is generally not subject to copyright in the United States and is credited to NASA ([NASA's terms](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/)).

**Map uncertainty.** The same `output_E.npy` contains `tmap_unc`: the standard deviation of the authors' sampled brightness-temperature maps, as computed by [ThERESA at 74a8fec, lines 207–210](https://github.com/rychallener/ThERESA/blob/74a8fec0462f4583e336bbc44e2f2441b263a49f/theresa/theresa.py#L207). Its 12 × 24 values range from 3.2717 to 9.5537 K (3.2717–9.5234 K in the shown cells). The uncertainty dataset uses a 0–10 K viridis scale, the same grid, orientation, bilinear display sampling and observed-longitude mask as the thermal map. This is uncertainty conditional on that fitted model: it excludes timing and model-choice uncertainty, and neighbouring cells are correlated. It is not a resolved measurement at every interpolated pixel. The deposit/paper run ambiguity described above applies to both maps.

**Hubble reflected light.** [Evans et al. (2013), Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.3239) reports HST/STIS G430L geometric albedos from a secondary eclipse on 20 December 2012 (GO-13006). [The factual transcription](source/science/evans-2013/albedo.json) retains all six bins from 290 to 570 nm, their flux-weighted central wavelengths, signed estimates and asymmetric 1σ errors. Horizontal bars show each bin's coverage; vertical bars show its published errors. In particular, −0.11 at 502 nm remains negative: it is a noisy fitted estimate, not negative physical reflectivity. The paper's two broader channels rebin the same observation and are not added as independent data. The chart leaves 570–780 nm empty. These sparse measurements do not supply a complete visible spectrum or an observed RGB globe.

**CHEOPS broadband albedo.** [Krenn et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07731) reports geometric albedo 0.076 ± 0.016 after subtracting the thermal contribution from thirteen occultations in 2021. [The transcription](source/science/krenn-2023/albedo.json) is drawn separately across the entire 350–1100 nm passband. Its error bar is placed at the centre for layout only; there is no monochromatic sample or inferred spectrum.

**Webb transmission spectrum.** [Fu et al. (2024)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07760-y) deposited the NIRCam data and retrievals as [Zenodo 11459715, version 1](https://zenodo.org/records/11459715), CC BY 4.0. From `HD189733b_JWST_data_model.zip`, the package retains two exact members under `zenodo/`:

- `Data/transit spectrum/HD189733b_NIRCam_spectrum.txt`: 139 rows of wavelength, wavelength half-width, transit depth `(Rp/Rs)^2` and depth uncertainty.
- `Model/Free/FULL_TD_median_result.txt`: 863 wavelength/depth rows of the deposited free-retrieval median model; 224 finite samples fall inside the 2.38–5.02 µm chart.

The chart multiplies fractional depths and their errors by 100 to show the percentage of starlight blocked. It keeps both filters' overlapping bins in the deposited order, draws their widths and 1σ errors, and connects only the deposited model samples. No refitting, rebinning, normalization or filter offsets are added. Missing model samples break the line; the archive's final NaN lies beyond the displayed range. The paper's atmospheric retrieval identifies water (H₂O), carbon dioxide (CO₂), carbon monoxide (CO) and hydrogen sulfide (H₂S). These are interpretations of the transmission spectrum, not spatial maps of individual gases.

All three charts are prepared SVGs from the shared [measured-spectrum reader and renderer](../../../packages/bake/src/objects/charts/measured-spectrum.ts). Their [recipes](source/content/charts.json) declare quantity, units, source members, expected row counts, display ranges and visible notes. Preparation refuses an axis that would clip a measurement or its errors. The browser displays the prepared images in the existing chart panel. Live inspection counted four HTML elements per chart and fourteen across the chart panel, with no inline SVG elements. The NIRCam chart is one external SVG image, so its 139 bins do not become individual page DOM elements. The [shared chart redesign](../../../docs/chart-recipes.md) adds the common axes, palette and translucent CHEOPS uncertainty region while preserving every published sample and error; the earlier source-value checks still apply, while the older screenshots show the previous style.

## Known problems

- **Eclipse maps of this planet depend on timing.** Its eclipse-only map moves about 0.5° to 1° of longitude per second of eclipse timing ([eclipse mapping](../../../docs/eclipse-mapping.md#timing-and-the-ramp-on-other-planets)). ThERESA models no light travel time across the orbit; with the 31 s it adds, the same data put the offset about 16° further west. The paper's 33° and the deposited map are shown unchanged; the dataset notes say the hot spot's longitude depends on timing.
- **The transit time's scale.** Table 1 labels the transit time UTC; the scene reads it as TDB. The data side with TDB: this project's TESS fit of the star (see [HD 189733](../hd-189733/README.md)) puts the transit 6.4 ± 0.9 s before this ephemeris, where a UTC time would be 69 s off. Either way the difference is 0.1% of an orbit and does not touch the map, which is shown as fitted.
- **Latitude.** The best model has no freedom in latitude; a 3-component model the paper does not prefer puts the hot spot 6.1 ± 1.6° from the equator. The deposited map's hottest cell is at +7.5°, one cell from the equator, and the map is nearly symmetric north to south.
- **North and south cannot be told apart.** An eclipse light curve cannot tell which side of the star the planet crosses; the orbit's construction fixes the map's north.
- **Only large patterns are real.** The 15° cells are the deposit's resolution; the bilinear sampling is display, not detail.
- **Brightness temperature, not temperature.** Each value is the temperature of a blackbody that would give the observed flux at 8 µm, against a PHOENIX model of the star.
- **Other maps of the same planet are not shown.** The deposit's SPARTA-reduction map (`output_S.npy`), earlier Spitzer eclipse maps and this project's map from raw exposures are in the [investigation ledger](investigations.json).
- **Assumptions of the frame.** Tidal locking and a pole on the orbit normal are assumed. The orbit's position angle on the sky is not measured by transits; it is set at 0 as a display convention. The planet is a sphere.

- **The Illustration dataset is art, not data.** Its colours, clouds and terrain are the artist's, and its longitudes are arbitrary; it is shown as NASA published it, with no colour corrected.
