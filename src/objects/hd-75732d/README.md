# 55 Cnc d

## Sources

55 Cnc d is the outermost of the 5 planets known around 55 Cnc. It was found in 2002 by the star's radial velocity and does not cross its star. Its orbit, tilt included, and its mass follow Xiao et al. (2026), "Transiting Planetary Systems with Distant Giant Companions Remain Moderately Coplanar" ([arXiv:2609.01377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01377)); its size is a model.

**Size and mass.** Radius 1.16 Jupiter radii from the NASA Exoplanet Archive's calculated radius (its Chen & Kipping 2017 mass-radius relationship): a model, not a measurement, since 55 Cnc d does not transit (https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/pscp_calc.html): 82,930.7 km at 71,492 km per Jupiter radius. GM from the mass 3.23 +/- 0.3 Jupiter masses (Xiao et al. (2026), Table 4: 3.23 (+0.29, -0.30) Jupiter masses, the true mass from radial velocities joined with Hipparcos and Gaia astrometry, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01377) times JPL's Jupiter GM. A sphere: no oblateness is measured.

**Orbit.** Table 4 of Xiao et al. (2026) gives the orbit from one joint fit of the star's radial velocities with Hipparcos and Gaia astrometry: period 5,084 ± 40 d; eccentricity 0.0153 (+0.0044, −0.0029); argument of periastron of the star's motion −156.2° (+2.8, −3.0), stored as 203.8°; longitude of the ascending node 124° (+28, −30), measured from north; inclination 93.9° (+6.9, −6.7); semi-major axis 5.58 au (+0.24, −0.26). The paper's angles are those of the Hipparcos and Gaia astrometric models (x to increasing right ascension, y to increasing declination, z away from the observer), which is the convention of the app's orbit record, so they are stored as printed. The semi-major axis is divided by the star's recorded radius of 656,045 km, which gives a/R* 1,272.4.

The paper prints no epoch for the planet's place along the orbit. The time of conjunction, 2451517.8 BJD (51517.3 BMJD_TDB, the planet in front of its star), is from Table 3 of Moutou et al. (2026), A&A 705, A190 ([arXiv:2510.11523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11523)), a fit of the radial velocities alone.

**Color.** No image or measured color exists. The neutral gray is lit by hd-75732's measured color (#ffe7d8, the color dataset of hd-75732 (src/objects/hd-75732/source/photometry/stellar-color.json)) at the gray's own brightness.

## Known problems

- **Place along the orbit.** The orbit's shape, size and plane are from one paper and its timing from another, whose period is 4,799 d against 5,084 d. Counted from the 1999 conjunction, the two periods put the second conjunction after it in March 2026 or October 2027, so the planet's place along its orbit today is uncertain by about 40°. Moutou et al. list six published periods between 4,799 and 5,574 d and say the spread is not independent of the star's other long-term signals.
- **Tilt.** An earlier estimate from Hubble's Fine Guidance Sensors gave 53° ± 6.8° from a small arc of the orbit (McArthur et al. 2004, ApJ 614, L81). The page draws the 2026 value, which rests on 25 years between Hipparcos and Gaia. The paper notes that such a fit may not tell a prograde orbit (under 90°) from a retrograde one (over 90°).
- **Size.** The radius of 1.16 Jupiter radii is the NASA Exoplanet Archive's value calculated from the mass. Nobody has measured the planet's size, because it does not transit.
- **Quoted text.** The introduction quotes sentences of the Wikipedia article "55 Cancri Ad" (revision 1377278261) verbatim, CC BY-SA 4.0.
