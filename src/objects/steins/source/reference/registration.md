# Coordinate and coverage interpretation

The pinned Stooke V3 map guide explicitly describes `steins_cylindrical_mosaic.jpg` as a 3600 × 1800 simple cylindrical image, north at the top, longitude 0 at center, increasing eastward. `steins_map_grid.jpg` independently shows the equator halfway down the cylinder and labels left-to-right 180, 270, 0, 90, 180. `steins_map_names.jpg` identifies the same terrain. Neither annotated map is sampled as appearance.

Preparation rolls the unannotated source half a turn to the runtime east-positive 0..360 convention; it neither flips rows nor estimates an alignment from appearance. Published map coordinates are retained. The Stooke map is controlled by Leyrat et al. (2010), whereas geometry is Jorda et al. (2012); the map is an aid to visualization, not a precision registration to each vertex of the later shape. Its explicitly revised interpretation near the south pole and the shape's published SPC/lightcurve seam remain. A visually plausible match is not a photogrammetric registration error bound.

The original JPEG is 8-bit grayscale with an embedded gray ICC profile. Shared preparation honors that profile when converting to sRGB. Its black exterior is not accompanied by a machine-readable validity mask. We use the existing north-edge-connected coverage traversal on sRGB display codes 0..2 inclusive, chosen to include minor JPEG variation in the nearly black exterior. Only connected exterior pixels are omitted; isolated black crater/shadow pixels remain. This heuristic can omit very dark terrain connected to exterior and retain ambiguous nonzero fill. It does not identify all cast shadows, does not infer unobserved terrain, and is not a scientific quality mask. The original is always preserved.

At native source resolution this classifies 3,567,208 of 6,480,000 map pixels (55.0495%) as exterior. Longitude/latitude map pixels are not equal-area surface samples; this percentage must not be called the fraction of the physical surface observed. Interpolation requires valid source coverage before the shared gray gap pattern is baked. The gap pattern never removes mesh geometry or cuts holes in context silhouettes.

The PDS shape is a right-handed Cartesian mesh in kilometers: +Z toward RA 91°, declination −62° (the catalog explicitly corrects the paper abstract), and +X through the provisional crater Spinel near the equator. This agrees with the positive pole in the pinned NAIF PCK00011. The newer IAU rotation uses W = 321.76 + 1428.09917 d at J2000, equivalent to about 6.050 hours; the shape paper/catalog reports the earlier 6.04681-hour solution. Fixed-epoch added Sun lighting uses the declared current IAU model and is approximate, rather than a reconstruction of the lighting in every photographed mosaic segment. Photographic illumination remains in Monochrome.
