# WASP-21 b

## Sources

It is the only planet known around Tangra. Its orbit and size follow Bouchy et al. 2010's fit, the archive's default. The introduction is generated from Bouchy et al. 2010's published values; the sections below are the data's own.

**Size and mass.** Radius 1.07 Jupiter radii from Bouchy et al. 2010 (2010arXiv1006.2605B), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010arXiv1006.2605B/abstract): 76,496.4 km at 71,492 km per Jupiter radius. GM from the mass 0.3 Jupiter masses (Bouchy et al. 2010, the mass the NASA Exoplanet Archive's composite table adopts (2010arXiv1006.2605B), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive, https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010arXiv1006.2605B/abstract) times JPL's Jupiter GM. A sphere: no oblateness is measured.

**Orbit.** Kokori et al. 2023 (2023ApJS..265....4K), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname KOKORI_ET_AL__2023): P 4.32250416 d Kokori et al. 2023 (2023ApJS..265....4K), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname KOKORI_ET_AL__2023): a/R* 10.54; Bouchy et al. 2010 (2010arXiv1006.2605B), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname BOUCHY_ET_AL__2010): inclination 88.75 degrees Bouchy et al. 2010 (2010arXiv1006.2605B), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname BOUCHY_ET_AL__2010): e 0 Kokori et al. 2023 (2023ApJS..265....4K), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname KOKORI_ET_AL__2023): transit mid-time 2457738.54027 BJD, taken as BJD_TDB; with its period, the row that predicts 2026-01-01 best (1 sigma 1 min) Display convention: transit photometry does not measure the orbit's position angle on the sky, so the ascending node is set at position angle 0 (celestial north).

**Color.** A black body at the 1,328 K dayside brightness temperature measured in secondary eclipse at 3.6 µm (Deming et al. 2023, dayside brightness temperature at 3.6 µm (uniform reanalysis of Spitzer's eclipses, CDS J/AJ/165/104 table 2)): #ff5d00. Chosen from the archive's emission rows by rule: 2 measured of 2 rows; the smallest relative uncertainty, then the longest wavelength. Reflected starlight is not included.

**Charts.** The orbits of Tangra's planets from above, from their hosted-orbit records, and its transit in 2 TESS sectors (56, 83), folded onto its orbit. Upper limits and rows without an error are left out.
