# Kepler-186 f

Kepler-186 f is 1.17 times as wide as Earth and circles its red dwarf every 130 days. Only its size and orbit are measured; here it is a plain gray sphere.

## Sources

Source selections, recorded trials and open questions are in the [investigation ledger](investigations.json).

Measured, from Torres et al. 2015, ApJ 800, 99 (2015), arXiv:1501.01101: radius 1.17 ± 0.08 Earth radii, period 129.9441 days, inclination 89.96 degrees, transit time (tau = BJD_UTC 2455789.4940 (the paper gives the time in UTC, not TDB; the difference of about a minute is not applied)). Scaled distance a/R* = 178.0. The orbit is drawn circular. The position angle of the orbit on the sky is not measured; the ascending node at celestial north is a display convention.

Not measured and not shown: mass, colour, albedo, surface, atmosphere, rotation. The rotation is assumed synchronous.

## Evidence

No dated test report exists for this body yet.

## Known problems

**Is it a planet?** Disputed. Torres et al. (2015) validated it. Burke et al. (2019, arXiv:1901.00506) find it "no longer meets the 1% criterion for statistical validation" and revise its planet probability to 96%; Robnik & Seljak (2025, arXiv:2509.07409) give it a 20% false-alarm probability and do not count it as validated. The package draws it as the validation paper describes it.

Kepler-186 has four more transiting planets (b, c, d, e); only f is represented here. Its scaled distance carries a large uncertainty (178 +65/-21). This planet is often described as potentially habitable; nothing here supports or shows that. The package draws a neutral gray sphere of the measured radius.
