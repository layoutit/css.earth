# TrES-1 b

## Sources

It is the only planet known around TrES-1. Its orbit and size follow Stassun et al. 2017's fit, the archive's default. The introduction is generated from Stassun et al. 2017's published values; the sections below are the data's own.

**Size and mass.** Radius 1.13 Jupiter radii from Stassun et al. 2017 (2017AJ....153..136S), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017AJ....153..136S/abstract): 80,786 km at 71,492 km per Jupiter radius. GM from the mass 0.84 Jupiter masses (Stassun et al. 2017, the mass the NASA Exoplanet Archive's composite table adopts (2017AJ....153..136S), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive, https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017AJ....153..136S/abstract) times JPL's Jupiter GM. A sphere: no oblateness is measured.

**Orbit.** Kokori et al. 2023 (2023ApJS..265....4K), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname KOKORI_ET_AL__2023): P 3.030069476 d Stassun et al. 2017 (2017AJ....153..136S), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname STASSUN_ET_AL__2017): a/R* 10.48; Stassun et al. 2017 (2017AJ....153..136S), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname STASSUN_ET_AL__2017): inclination 90 degrees Stassun et al. 2017 (2017AJ....153..136S), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname STASSUN_ET_AL__2017): e 0 Kokori et al. 2023 (2023ApJS..265....4K), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname KOKORI_ET_AL__2023): transit mid-time 2456822.891157 BJD, taken as BJD_TDB; with its period, the row that predicts 2026-01-01 best (1 sigma 1 min) Display convention: transit photometry does not measure the orbit's position angle on the sky, so the ascending node is set at position angle 0 (celestial north).

**Color.** A black body at the 1,010 K dayside brightness temperature measured in secondary eclipse at 4.5 µm (Charbonneau et al. 2005, dayside brightness temperature at 4.5 µm (NASA Exoplanet Archive emission table)): #ff3100. Chosen from the archive's emission rows by rule: 7 measured of 7 rows; the smallest relative uncertainty, then the longest wavelength. Reflected starlight is not included.

**Charts.** The orbits of TrES-1's planets from above, from their hosted-orbit records, and its dayside emission, 4 bins from Cubillos et al. 2014 in the archive's emissionspec table; its transit in 3 TESS sectors (74, 80, 81), folded onto its orbit. Upper limits and rows without an error are left out.
