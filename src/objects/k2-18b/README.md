# K2-18 b

K2-18 b is a sub-Neptune that transits the red dwarf K2-18, 38 parsecs away.

## Sources

Source selections, recorded trials and open questions are in the [investigation ledger](investigations.json).

Measured, from Benneke et al. (2019, ApJL 887, L14; arXiv:1909.04642), Table 1: radius 2.610 ± 0.087 Earth radii (0.4445 solar-radius star), period 32.940045 days, transit time BJD_UTC 2457725.551189, semi-major axis 0.15910 au and impact parameter 0.641, which give a/R* = 76.97 and inclination 89.5228 degrees; mass 8.63 ± 1.35 Earth masses (Cloutier et al. 2019, as listed there). This revises the discovery-era values (Sarkis et al. 2018, whose radius, period, transit time and inclination are Benneke et al. 2017's). Eight JWST transits of 2023–2025 fall 8.5 to 12.0 minutes before this ephemeris (measured here from MAST white-light curves); the orbit is not corrected for that. From Sarkis et al. (2018) Table 3 (joint radial-velocity fit): eccentricity 0.20 ± 0.08 and argument of periastron −0.10 (+0.81/−0.59) rad, drawn as an ellipse; the angle is loosely constrained. The position angle of the orbit on the sky is not measured; the ascending node at celestial north is a display convention.

Not measured and not shown: colour, albedo, surface or clouds, rotation. The rotation is assumed synchronous. The mass is adopted from Cloutier et al. (2019).

## Known problems

K2-18 b is often described as a candidate ocean or habitable world. Nothing here supports or shows that: the package draws a neutral gray sphere of the measured radius and makes no claim about its surface. Its atmosphere has been observed in transmission, first with Hubble (water vapour, Benneke et al. 2019) and since with JWST; no atmosphere is drawn.
