# WASP-28 b

## Sources

It is the only planet known around WASP-28. Its orbit and size follow Mo&#x10D;nik et al. 2020's fit, the archive's default. The introduction is generated from Mo&#x10D;nik et al. 2020's published values; the sections below are the data's own.

**Size and mass.** Radius 1.219 Jupiter radii from Mo&#x10D;nik et al. 2020 (2020PASP..132a4401M), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020PASP..132a4401M/abstract): 87,148.7 km at 71,492 km per Jupiter radius. GM from the mass 0.889 Jupiter masses (Mo&#x10D;nik et al. 2020, the mass the NASA Exoplanet Archive's composite table adopts (2020PASP..132a4401M), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive, https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020PASP..132a4401M/abstract) times JPL's Jupiter GM. A sphere: no oblateness is measured.

**Orbit.** Mo&#x10D;nik et al. 2020 (2020PASP..132a4401M), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname MO__X10D_NIK_ET_AL__2020): P 3.40883495 d Mo&#x10D;nik et al. 2020 (2020PASP..132a4401M), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname MO__X10D_NIK_ET_AL__2020): a/R* derived from its semi-major axis 0.0442 au and stellar radius 1.083 solar radii; Mo&#x10D;nik et al. 2020 (2020PASP..132a4401M), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname MO__X10D_NIK_ET_AL__2020): inclination 88.514 degrees Mo&#x10D;nik et al. 2020 (2020PASP..132a4401M), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname MO__X10D_NIK_ET_AL__2020): e 0 Mo&#x10D;nik et al. 2020 (2020PASP..132a4401M), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname MO__X10D_NIK_ET_AL__2020): transit mid-time 2457642.502126 BJD, taken as BJD_TDB; with its period, the row that predicts 2026-01-01 best (1 sigma 1 min) Display convention: transit photometry does not measure the orbit's position angle on the sky, so the ascending node is set at position angle 0 (celestial north).

**Color.** A black body at the 1,548 K dayside brightness temperature measured in secondary eclipse at 4.5 µm (Deming et al. 2023, dayside brightness temperature at 4.5 µm (uniform reanalysis of Spitzer's eclipses, CDS J/AJ/165/104 table 2)): #ff7000. Chosen from the archive's emission rows by rule: 2 measured of 2 rows; the smallest relative uncertainty, then the longest wavelength. Reflected starlight is not included.

**Charts.** The orbits of WASP-28's planets from above, from their hosted-orbit records, and its transit in 3 TESS sectors (42, 70, 92), folded onto its orbit. Upper limits and rows without an error are left out.
