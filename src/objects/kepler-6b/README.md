# Kepler-6 b

## Sources

It is the only planet known around Kepler-6. Its orbit and size follow Esteves et al. 2015's fit, the archive's default. The introduction is generated from Esteves et al. 2015's published values; the sections below are the data's own.

**Size and mass.** Radius 1.304 Jupiter radii from Esteves et al. 2015 (2015ApJ...804..150E), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...804..150E/abstract): 93,225.6 km at 71,492 km per Jupiter radius. GM from the mass 0.668 Jupiter masses (Esteves et al. 2015, the mass the NASA Exoplanet Archive's composite table adopts (2015ApJ...804..150E), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive, https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...804..150E/abstract) times JPL's Jupiter GM. A sphere: no oblateness is measured.

**Orbit.** Holczer et al. 2016 (2016ApJS..225....9H), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname HOLCZER_ET_AL__2016): P 3.23469918 d Esteves et al. 2015 (2015ApJ...804..150E), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname ESTEVES_ET_AL__2015): a/R* 7.503; Esteves et al. 2015 (2015ApJ...804..150E), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname ESTEVES_ET_AL__2015): inclination 88.93 degrees Dunham et al. 2010 (2010ApJ...713L.136D), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname DUNHAM_ET_AL__2010): e 0 Holczer et al. 2016 (2016ApJS..225....9H), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname HOLCZER_ET_AL__2016): transit mid-time 2454954.486657 BJD, taken as BJD_TDB; with its period, the row that predicts 2026-01-01 best (1 sigma 1 min) Display convention: transit photometry does not measure the orbit's position angle on the sky, so the ascending node is set at position angle 0 (celestial north).

**Color.** A black body at the 1,700 K dayside brightness temperature measured in secondary eclipse at 4.5 µm (Désert et al. 2011, dayside brightness temperature at 4.5 µm (NASA Exoplanet Archive emission table)): #ff7a00. Chosen from the archive's emission rows by rule: 2 measured of 2 rows; the smallest relative uncertainty, then the longest wavelength. Reflected starlight is not included.

**Charts.** The orbits of Kepler-6's planets from above, from their hosted-orbit records, and its transit in 3 TESS sectors (56, 74, 75), folded onto its orbit. Upper limits and rows without an error are left out.
