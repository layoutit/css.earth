# K2-31 b

## Sources

It is the only planet known around K2-31. Its orbit and size follow Grziwa et al. 2016's fit, the archive's default. The introduction is generated from Grziwa et al. 2016's published values; the sections below are the data's own.

**Size and mass.** Radius 1.06 Jupiter radii from Grziwa et al. 2016 (2016AJ....152..132G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016AJ....152..132G/abstract): 75,781.5 km at 71,492 km per Jupiter radius. GM from the mass 1.774 Jupiter masses (Grziwa et al. 2016, the mass the NASA Exoplanet Archive's composite table adopts (2016AJ....152..132G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive, https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016AJ....152..132G/abstract) times JPL's Jupiter GM. A sphere: no oblateness is measured.

**Orbit.** Kokori et al. 2023 (2023ApJS..265....4K), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname KOKORI_ET_AL__2023): P 1.25784887 d Grziwa et al. 2016 (2016AJ....152..132G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname GRZIWA_ET_AL__2016): a/R* 6.05; Grziwa et al. 2016 (2016AJ....152..132G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname GRZIWA_ET_AL__2016): inclination 79.9 degrees Grziwa et al. 2016 (2016AJ....152..132G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname GRZIWA_ET_AL__2016): e 0 Kokori et al. 2023 (2023ApJS..265....4K), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname KOKORI_ET_AL__2023): transit mid-time 2456952.717941 BJD, taken as BJD_TDB; with its period, the row that predicts 2026-01-01 best (1 sigma 4 min) Display convention: transit photometry does not measure the orbit's position angle on the sky, so the ascending node is set at position angle 0 (celestial north).

**Color.** An estimate, not a measurement: a black body at the 1,688 K equilibrium temperature of Howard et al. 2025, equilibrium temperature (NASA Exoplanet Archive planetary systems table): #ff7900. Chosen by rule: 1 paper in the archive prints one; the most recently published. On 120 hot Jupiters whose day side Spitzer measured, the measured temperature is within 20% of this kind of estimate for 83% (Deming et al. 2023); see [the expected glow](../../../docs/color-preparation.md#the-expected-glow-of-a-hot-giant). Reflected starlight is not included.

**Charts.** The orbits of K2-31's planets from above, from their hosted-orbit records, and its transit in 1 TESS sector (91), folded onto its orbit. Upper limits and rows without an error are left out.
