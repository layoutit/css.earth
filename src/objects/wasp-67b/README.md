# WASP-67 b

## Sources

It is the only planet known around WASP-67. Its orbit and size follow Stassun et al. 2017's fit, the archive's default. This account was drafted from Stassun et al. 2017's values; the sections below are the data's own.

**Size and mass.** Radius 1.15 Jupiter radii from Stassun et al. 2017 (2017AJ....153..136S), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017AJ....153..136S/abstract): 82,215.8 km at 71,492 km per Jupiter radius. GM from the mass 0.43 Jupiter masses (Stassun et al. 2017, the mass the NASA Exoplanet Archive's composite table adopts (2017AJ....153..136S), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive, https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017AJ....153..136S/abstract) times JPL's Jupiter GM. A sphere: no oblateness is measured.

**Orbit.** Kokori et al. 2022 (2022ApJS..258...40K), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname KOKORI_ET_AL__2022): P 4.6144166 d Stassun et al. 2017 (2017AJ....153..136S), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname STASSUN_ET_AL__2017): a/R* 12.83; Stassun et al. 2017 (2017AJ....153..136S), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname STASSUN_ET_AL__2017): inclination 85.8 degrees Stassun et al. 2017 (2017AJ....153..136S), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname STASSUN_ET_AL__2017): e 0 Kokori et al. 2022 (2022ApJS..258...40K), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname KOKORI_ET_AL__2022): transit mid-time 2456618.0537 BJD, taken as BJD_TDB; with its period, the row that predicts 2026-01-01 best (1 sigma 1 min) Display convention: transit photometry does not measure the orbit's position angle on the sky, so the ascending node is set at position angle 0 (celestial north).

**Colour.** A black body at the 1,015 K dayside brightness temperature measured in secondary eclipse at 4.5 µm (Kammer et al. 2015, dayside brightness temperature at 4.5 µm (NASA Exoplanet Archive emission table)): #ff3200. Chosen from the archive's emission rows by rule: 2 measured of 2 rows; the smallest relative uncertainty, then the longest wavelength. Reflected starlight is not included.

**Charts.** The orbits of WASP-67's planets from above, from their hosted-orbit records, and its transit in 1 TESS sector (92), folded onto its orbit. Upper limits and rows without an error are left out.
