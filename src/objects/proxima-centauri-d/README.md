# Proxima Centauri d

## Sources

Proxima Centauri d is the innermost of the two planets known around the nearest star to the Sun. It is known only from the star's radial velocity and does not cross its star. **Its tilt is not measured.** The page draws it in the star's equatorial plane, as an approximate placement; its period, timing, orbit size and minimum mass follow Suárez Mascareño et al. (2025), "Diving into the planetary system of Proxima with NIRPS", A&A ([doi:10.1051/0004-6361/202553728](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202553728)), and its size is a model.

**Size and mass.** Radius 0.0618 Jupiter radii from the NASA Exoplanet Archive's calculated radius (its Chen & Kipping 2017 mass-radius relationship): a model, not a measurement, since Proxima Centauri d does not transit (https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/pscp_calc.html): 4,418.2 km at 71,492 km per Jupiter radius. GM from the mass 0.000818 +/- 0.00012 Jupiter masses (Suárez Mascareño et al. (2025), A&A, doi:10.1051/0004-6361/202553728, Table 3: minimum mass (m sin i) 0.26 +/- 0.038 Earth masses from the star's radial velocities; the same table gives 0.357 +/- 0.072 Earth masses for an orbit in the star's equatorial plane, tilted 47 degrees, https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202553728) times JPL's Jupiter GM. A sphere: no oblateness is measured.

**Orbit.** Table 3 of Suárez Mascareño et al. (2025), a fit of the star's radial velocities from HARPS, ESPRESSO and NIRPS with the eccentricity fixed to zero, gives the orbit within its plane: period 5.12338 ± 0.00035 d; time of inferior conjunction 2460557.55 ± 0.16 BJD (60557.05 BMJD_TDB, the planet nearest the observer); semi-major axis 0.02881 ± 0.00017 au, divided by the star's recorded radius of 98,094 km to give a/R* 43.94.

**Assumed plane.** Radial velocities do not give an orbit's tilt, and no paper measures Proxima Centauri d's. The record sets the tilt to 47°, the tilt of the star's rotation axis that Klein et al. (2021), MNRAS 500, 1844 measure as 47° ± 7° from the star's magnetic field: that is, it assumes the planet orbits in the star's equatorial plane. Suárez Mascareño et al. print the planet's mass under the same assumption. This page cites no test of that assumption for a planet like this one; Garraffo et al. (2022) note that the planet's tilt to the star's axis is not known. The direction of the orbit on the sky is a display convention (ascending node at position angle 0). The orbit record says so (`placement: approximate`), and the app shows it as it shows a moon whose orbit is not uniquely known: the path is dashed and the planet is named "(approx)".

**Color.** No image or measured color exists. The neutral gray is lit by proxima-centauri's measured color (#ffd06e, the color dataset of proxima-centauri (src/objects/proxima-centauri/source/photometry/stellar-color.json)) at the gray's own brightness.

## Known problems

- **Assumed tilt.** See "Assumed plane" above. If the planet does not orbit in the star's equatorial plane, the orbit drawn here is tilted wrongly by an unknown angle.
- **Mass.** 0.260 ± 0.038 Earth masses is the minimum mass (m sin i) from radial velocities. For the assumed 47° the paper gives 0.357 ± 0.072 Earth masses.
- **Size.** The radius is the NASA Exoplanet Archive's value calculated from the mass. Nobody has measured the planet's size, because it does not transit.
- **Quoted text.** The introduction quotes sentences of the Wikipedia article "Proxima Centauri d" (revision 1374087109) verbatim, CC BY-SA 4.0.
