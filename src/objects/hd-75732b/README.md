# 55 Cnc b

## Sources

55 Cnc b is one of the 5 planets known around 55 Cnc. It was found in 1996 by the star's radial velocity and does not cross its star. **Its tilt is not measured.** The page draws it in the plane measured for 55 Cnc d, on the assumption that the planets share one plane; its orbit within that plane follows Moutou et al. (2026), A&A 705, A190 ([arXiv:2510.11523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11523)), and its size is a model.

**Size and mass.** Radius 1.24 Jupiter radii from the NASA Exoplanet Archive's calculated radius (its Chen & Kipping 2017 mass-radius relationship): a model, not a measurement, since 55 Cnc b does not transit (https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/pscp_calc.html): 88,650.1 km at 71,492 km per Jupiter radius. GM from the mass 0.8684 +/- 0.0283 Jupiter masses (Moutou et al. (2026), A&A 705, A190, Table 3: minimum mass (m sin i) 276 +/- 9 Earth masses from the star's radial velocities; at the assumed tilt of 93.9 degrees the mass is 0.2% more, https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11523) times JPL's Jupiter GM. A sphere: no oblateness is measured.

**Orbit.** Table 3 of Moutou et al. (2026), a fit of the star's radial velocities, gives the orbit within its plane: period 14.651552 ± 0.000036 d; time of conjunction 2455495.595 (+0.012, −0.015) BJD (55495.095 BMJD_TDB, the planet nearest the observer); eccentricity 0.0029 (+0.0027, −0.0020); argument of periastron of the star's motion 0.41 (+0.97, −1.0) rad, stored as 23.5°; semi-major axis 0.118 ± 0.002 au, divided by the star's recorded radius of 656,045 km to give a/R* 26.91.

**Assumed plane.** Radial velocities do not give an orbit's tilt or its direction on the sky, and no paper measures either for 55 Cnc b. The record takes both from 55 Cnc d: inclination 93.9° (+6.9, −6.7) and ascending node 124° (+28, −30), which Xiao et al. (2026, [arXiv:2609.01377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01377)) measure from radial velocities joined with Hipparcos and Gaia astrometry. That paper finds 55 Cnc d within 3.9° (+6.9, −6.7) of the tilt of the transiting planet 55 Cnc e and calls the system consistent with a nearly coplanar architecture. Across its 19 systems the paper finds that the tilts of inner and outer planets differ by a Rayleigh scale of 15.8° (+2.8, −2.6), far from random orientations. That 55 Cnc b lies in the same plane is this page's assumption, not a result of either paper. The orbit record says so (`placement: approximate`), and the app shows it as it shows a moon whose orbit is not uniquely known: the path is dashed and the planet is named "(approx)".

## Known problems

- **Assumed tilt and node.** See "Assumed plane" above: the orbit's plane is 55 Cnc d's, not a measurement of this planet. If the planets do not share one plane, the orbit drawn here is turned by an unknown angle.
- **Mass.** 276 ± 9 Earth masses is the minimum mass (m sin i) from radial velocities. At the assumed tilt the mass is 0.2% more; at another tilt it is larger.
- **Size.** The radius is the NASA Exoplanet Archive's value calculated from the mass. Nobody has measured the planet's size, because it does not transit.
- **Quoted text.** The introduction quotes sentences of the Wikipedia article "55 Cancri Ab" (revision 1375884878) verbatim, CC BY-SA 4.0.
