It is the only planet known around WASP-75. Its orbit and size follow Gomez et al. 2013's fit, the archive's default. The introduction is generated from Gomez et al. 2013's published values; the sections below are the data's own.

**Size and mass.** Radius 1.27 Jupiter radii from Gomez et al. 2013 (2013A&A...559A..36G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013A%26A...559A..36G/abstract): 90,794.8 km at 71,492 km per Jupiter radius. GM from the mass 1.07 Jupiter masses (Gomez et al. 2013, the mass the NASA Exoplanet Archive's composite table adopts (2013A&A...559A..36G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive, https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013A%26A...559A..36G/abstract) times JPL's Jupiter GM. A sphere: no oblateness is measured.

**Orbit.** Kokori et al. 2023 (2023ApJS..265....4K), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname KOKORI_ET_AL__2023): P 2.4841976 d Gomez et al. 2013 (2013A&A...559A..36G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname GOMEZ_ET_AL__2013): a/R* derived from its semi-major axis 0.0375 au and stellar radius 1.26 solar radii; Gomez et al. 2013 (2013A&A...559A..36G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname GOMEZ_ET_AL__2013): inclination 82 degrees Gomez et al. 2013 (2013A&A...559A..36G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname GOMEZ_ET_AL__2013): e 0 Kokori et al. 2023 (2023ApJS..265....4K), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname KOKORI_ET_AL__2023): transit mid-time 2457131.671596 BJD, taken as BJD_TDB; with its period, the row that predicts 2026-01-01 best (1 sigma 1 min) Display convention: transit photometry does not measure the orbit's position angle on the sky, so the ascending node is set at position angle 0 (celestial north).

**Color.** A black body at the 1,397 K dayside brightness temperature measured in secondary eclipse at 4.5 µm (Deming et al. 2023, dayside brightness temperature at 4.5 µm (uniform reanalysis of Spitzer's eclipses, CDS J/AJ/165/104 table 2)): #ff6300. Chosen from the archive's emission rows by rule: 1 measured of 1 rows; the smallest relative uncertainty, then the longest wavelength. Reflected starlight is not included.

**Charts.** The orbits of WASP-75's planets from above, from their hosted-orbit records, and its transit in 3 TESS sectors (42, 70, 92), folded onto its orbit. Upper limits and rows without an error are left out.
