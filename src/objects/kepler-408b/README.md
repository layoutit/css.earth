# Kepler-408 b

## Sources

It is the only planet known around Kepler-408. Its orbit and size follow Marcy et al. 2014's fit, the archive's default. This account was drafted from Marcy et al. 2014's values; the sections below are the data's own.

**Size and mass.** Radius 0.073 Jupiter radii from Marcy et al. 2014 (2014ApJS..210...20M), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJS..210...20M/abstract): 5,218.9 km at 71,492 km per Jupiter radius. No mass is measured: Marcy et al. 2014 (2014ApJS..210...20M), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJS..210...20M/abstract) gives only an upper limit of 0.01573168 Jupiter masses, so GM is 0, the records' unpublished value. A sphere: no oblateness is measured.

**Orbit.** Gajdoš et al. 2019 (2019RAA....19...41G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname GAJDO_SCARON__ET_AL__2019): P 2.4650455167 d Marcy et al. 2014 (2014ApJS..210...20M), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname MARCY_ET_AL__2014): a/R* derived by Kepler's third law from its period 2.4650455167 d, stellar mass 1.08 and radius 1.23 solar units; Q1-Q12 KOI Table, via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname Q1_Q12_KOI_TABLE): inclination 88.24 degrees Weiss et al. 2024 (2024ApJS..270....8W), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname WEISS_ET_AL__2024): e 0 Gajdoš et al. 2019 (2019RAA....19...41G), via the NASA Exoplanet Archive ps table (pl_refname GAJDO_SCARON__ET_AL__2019): transit mid-time 2454965.67136 BJD, taken as BJD_TDB; with its period, the row that predicts 2026-01-01 best (1 sigma 5 min) Display convention: transit photometry does not measure the orbit's position angle on the sky, so the ascending node is set at position angle 0 (celestial north).

**Colour.** No image or measured colour exists. The neutral gray is lit by kepler-408's measured colour (#faf4ff, the colour lens of kepler-408 (src/objects/kepler-408/source/photometry/stellar-color.json)) at the gray's own brightness.
